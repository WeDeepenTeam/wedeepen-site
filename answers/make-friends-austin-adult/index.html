How to Make Friends in Austin as an Adult

By WeDeepen · October 3, 2026

The short answer

To make friends in Austin as an adult, pick one or two recurring activities and keep showing up, then turn familiar faces into specific plans: a league through Austin Sports & Social Club, a run club, a Meetup group, a Timeleft dinner, or Bumble For Friends. Research suggests casual friendship takes about 50 hours together. If you want deeper conversation from the start, WeDeepen's community is built around it.

Making friends as an adult in Austin has a specific shape. Many people here are transplants, plenty work remotely, and the friends from school or your last city are a flight away. None of that means something is wrong with you. Adult friendship just doesn't happen by accident the way it did at school, so you have to build the conditions for it on purpose.

Friendship takes time, so plan for it

University of Kansas researcher Jeffrey Hall estimated the hours it takes: about 50 hours together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, about 90 to become friends, and more than 200 to become close friends. You won't get there through one happy hour. You get there by putting yourself somewhere you'll see the same people every week.

Where adults in Austin make friends

A recurring team or league

Austin Sports & Social Club runs coed adult leagues in about ten sports, with games in neighborhoods across the city and a social hour afterward. You can sign up alone and get placed on a team.

A run club

Austin Runners Club offers weekly training and social runs, and the Austin Insider running guide covers the trails and groups at the heart of the city's running scene. Runs are easy to drop into, and coffee afterward is where friendships start.

A Meetup group built for newcomers

Meetup's Austin social category includes groups made specifically for people new to the city or looking for friends. Smaller groups that meet weekly are where you'll start recognizing people.

A friendship app

Bumble For Friends works like a dating app for platonic connection. It helps you find people; turning a match into a friend still takes repeated plans in person.

A table of strangers

Timeleft books you into a weekly dinner with a small group matched by personality. One dinner won't make a friend, but it's good practice, and some tables keep meeting.

People come to get better at love in all its forms: friendship, dating, partnership, and the relationship with themselves. Plenty come on their own.

From acquaintance to friend

  • Be the one who invites. Most people want more friends and wait for someone else to make the plan. Make it.
  • Make specific plans. "Want to get tacos after Thursday's run?" works better than "We should hang out sometime."
  • Follow up within a week. Momentum matters more than polish.
  • Go a level deeper. Share something true about your week, not just the highlights. Closeness grows when both people risk a little.
  • Stop shrinking. If you mute yourself to seem easygoing, people meet a version of you that isn't real. Let them meet the real one.

FAQ

How do I make friends in Austin as an adult?

Join one or two recurring activities, like a league, run club, Meetup group, or community such as WeDeepen, then turn repeat faces into specific plans. Research suggests friendship takes dozens of hours together, so consistency matters most.

Is it normal to struggle to make friends after moving to Austin?

Yes. Many Austin residents moved here as adults or work remotely, so lots of people are in the same position, which makes newcomer groups easier to join.

What are good friend-making activities in Austin for introverts?

Small recurring groups work best: a book club, a volunteer shift, a class, or WeDeepen's online Tuesday session, where you can listen first and speak when you're ready.
