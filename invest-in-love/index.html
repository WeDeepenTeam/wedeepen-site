The Thesis

Each decade chooses its frontier.
Love is next.

Fitness went mainstream in the 1980s. Yoga in the late ’90s. Meditation and mindfulness through the 2000s. Biohacking through the 2010s.

The pattern is consistent. A generation discovers that a dimension of being human is trainable, worth studying, worthy of real practice. Teachers, books, platforms, and communities form around it. The category grows up. Early platforms compound into category-defining companies.

The teachers in love already exist. Esther Perel cracked the conversation open. The Hendricks, John Gray, Ken Page, Annie Lalla, Jillian Turecki, and dozens of brilliant practitioners are doing the work in adjacent silos. What’s missing is the connective tissue — the platform where the field’s best teachers find each other, find the people ready to learn, and shape what this becomes.

WeDeepen is being built to be that platform.

Why Now

The cultural conditions for this category have already arrived.

Most of the people positioned to fund this work are also exactly the people built for it.

Loneliness as a public-health crisis

The U.S. Surgeon General has named loneliness an epidemic with health effects on par with smoking. Marriage rates are at historic lows. Demand for repair, intimacy, and skill-based relating is structural, not cyclical.

Dating apps have failed the next layer

Match Group’s decline and rising user fatigue point to the same gap. Apps optimized for matching never taught anyone how to love. The customer is now ready to pay for the teaching, not the swipe.

Wellness has trained the buyer

A generation that already pays for therapy, retreats, breathwork, plant medicine, and biohacking is fluent in “practice.” They are pre-qualified for love-as-practice — the next adjacency in a $5T+ wellness market.

No category leader exists yet

Authors, podcasters, and individual coaches are siloed. There is no Peloton, no Headspace, no MasterClass for love. The brand, the platform, and the practitioner network around this work are unbuilt.

We are not predicting the trend. We are catching it at the moment the buyer, the teachers, and the absence of a leader are all true at once.

The Competitive Landscape

Each gets one piece. None has the full stack.

Investors will name the obvious comparables. Here’s how they actually map — and why the gap is the opportunity.

Esther Perel & Creator Brands

The audience without the platform.

Massive podcast and book reach, world-class brand. But it’s content, not infrastructure. No practitioner network, no marketplace, no membership. WeDeepen is what someone in that audience does next.

Calm / Headspace / Wellness Platforms

The platform without the category.

Built the muscle for “practice as subscription” and trained millions of buyers. But they’re optimized for individual mindfulness — different posture, different practitioner type. A “love” module doesn’t make them a love company.

Match Group / Bumble / Dating Apps

The userbase without the alignment.

Hundreds of millions of users. But the business model is engagement — keeping people on the app — not transformation. The brand baggage is real. Their up-market pivots are bandages on a model that already failed the customer.

BetterHelp / Talkspace / Online Therapy

The service without the community.

Therapists on tap. But it’s individual, transactional, clinical. No brand, no peer room, no rating system, no curation. WeDeepen is community, brand, curation, and service in one stack.

Local Couples Therapy + Retreats + Workshops

The supply without the brand.

This work happens everywhere — in a thousand silos, with no quality signal, no distribution, no network. WeDeepen is the platform layer that organizes the supply, certifies quality, aggregates demand, and makes the practitioners discoverable.

Five comparables. Five missing pieces. WeDeepen is being built to be the one with all five.

Risks We’re Underwriting

What we’d want you to ask first.

We’d rather you ask the hard questions now than after you wire. Here are the four risks we think about most — and how we plan to handle each.

Risk 1

Founder concentration

The risk: Christina is the brand. Burnout, illness, distraction = company risk.

How we handle it:

This round funds the team that lets her be a founder, not a sole operator. The Strategist marketplace decentralizes brand load over 18 months. Frameworks and IP convert from her head into platform-owned assets. By month 18, the company is durable beyond any one person.

Risk 2

Marketplace cold-start

The risk: Two-sided platforms die in the chicken-and-egg phase.

How we handle it:

We start past it. Twelve founding Strategists already engaged. An existing Love Club community gives demand-side warmth from day one. Immersions create acute moments where supply and demand meet in the same room and convert in real time.

Risk 3

Category-education timeline

The risk: Convincing people that love is “trainable” and worth premium pricing is a multi-year story.

How we handle it:

The buyer is already pre-trained by the wellness category. Premium pricing is already proven — three Love Club Plus memberships sold in April 2026 at $9,997 with no paid acquisition. Renewal data over the next 12 months will validate the model publicly.

Risk 4

Marketplace economics at scale

The risk: 10% take rate is small; operationally complex to track at scale.

How we handle it:

Subscription revenue from Strategists fronts the cash flow, decoupling us from take-rate fragility. AI-leveraged platform handles attribution at any scale. The 10% take rate compounds as a long-term layer, not the foundation.

These aren’t the only risks. They’re the four we’d ask if we were on your side of the table. The full risk register is in the data room.
